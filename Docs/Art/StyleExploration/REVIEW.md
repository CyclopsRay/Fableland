# Realm style exploration — art-direction review

*2026-11-25. Covers all 78 concept images in `img/` (34 from GPT Image 2.5, 44 from Midjourney v8.2), read against the brief in `README.md`, the style bible (§0, §2.4, §2.6, §2.8) and what ships today. The gallery for choosing is `index.html` in this folder. Nothing here changes a shipped asset.*

**How to read it.** Nine worlds in the brief's order, then the overworld. For each: every image in a sentence or two; whether the arena reads; what a kit would take; a verdict with a pick and a runner-up. "A, in Midjourney's handling" means direction A as the Midjourney variants paint it, with the best variant named. The last section has the summary table, where picks collide, what else needs deciding, and sharper prompts for a second round.

**How it was looked at.** GPT Image pictures at preview size; Midjourney variants side by side at about 780 px each; four crops from the full-size originals to check mark quality. Stills cannot show motion, the game's own characters, or the game's resolution — see the end.

## Across the batch

- **The two models fail in opposite directions.** GPT Image obeyed the composition every time — side view, one ground, three or four ledges, two fighters — and carried unusual media very well (linocut, etching, collage, embroidery, codex, manuscript, silhouette). But its resting state is dense premium key art: wherever the medium was painterly (VK-A, HC-A, PL-A, PL-C, BM-C, TF-B) it filled every corner, which is the opposite of the calm a fighter needs. Midjourney ignored the frame — all 44 are square, several are top-down or in perspective, and three or four children sometimes turn up instead of two fighters — but it paints sparsely, kindly and close to a kit. In four worlds its version of direction A is the one to follow.
- **Stock furniture to ignore.** Almost every GPT Image arena has the same floating island trailing roots, rags or banners, an arched bridge for ground, several waterfalls (even under the sea) and a large moon. These are the model's habits, not ideas about the world.
- **One trick kept turning up in the images that read best: a light cap on every walkable top.** Icing (VK-D), moss (HC-C, TF-A), a red lip (TF-C), a pale stitched top (CC-B), bare paper (BM-A), a lit plane (SL-A). It should become a house rule.
- **Things the models added that must not go forward:** religious figures (HC-D, PL-B), blood (KH-A), lettering (PL-A-mj1, PL-A-mj2, OW-A-mj2), frames and borders (VK-A-mj1, TD-A-mj3, CC-A-mj1, CC-A-mj4, TD-D), and a star where a pentagon was asked for (OW-A-mj4 and all four OW-B Midjourney variants).

## VK — VanillaKindom · Beach

**Ships today:** a watercolour sticker sheet (`unity_beach_design_sheet.png`) — sun, clouds, wave, watch-tower, each a soft wash inside a pencil-brown line with a white die-cut edge. The marks are already hand-made; the stiffness is in how the stickers sit on the grid, not in how they are painted.

### Image by image

- **VK-A — Storybook watercolour & ink (GPT Image 2.5).** A dense cove: stilt huts left and right, a beached wreck in the middle, mossy rocks and tide pools across the whole foreground, cliffs and a lighthouse behind. Handsome, but it is not the storybook medium — it is tight JRPG concept illustration with a paper texture laid over it: the line never breaks, nothing blooms, every centimetre is rendered. The ground is seen from above (pools in front of and behind the fighters), so there is a beach, not a walking line.
- **VK-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** The loosest wash and the truest picture-book hand of the four — and the wrong picture: a three-quarter view of a beach from above, two children facing the reader, no ledges, and a hand-drawn border round the sheet although the prompt forbade one.
- **VK-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The only one of the four that is an arena: straight-on, sea horizon behind, driftwood planks floating at three heights, two trestle decks and a boat to stand on, a stilt hut closing the right side, two very small fighters on the sand. Cloud wash and bare paper do most of the work. The sand is still a plane with pools in it rather than one line.
- **VK-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The same vocabulary with the best ground of the set: a single inked line with a coral-red wash bleeding below it, like a cut through the beach. The floating planks are thin and nearly identical — the repeated stamp creeping back — and the fighters are toddlers tucked in a corner.
- **VK-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** A beach seen from above with an L-shaped pier, and under it a slab of sand the two children stand on like a stage block. The slab with its red underside is a usable idea for ground; everything else is the wrong view and there is nothing to jump to.
- **VK-B — Mid-century gouache picture book (GPT Image 2.5).** Golden hour: pink sky, a low sun on the water, a striped lighthouse, and a natural rock bridge whose flat sandy top is the ground; a floating turf island, a timber gantry and a rock shelf are the ledges. Flat shapes, dry-brush edges, no outline — but "mid-century" was ignored: the palms and rocks are naturalistic and the glow is airbrushed, so it lands on the modern indie-game key-art look, not a chalky 1950s picture book. It is the most playable image of this world.
- **VK-C — Torn-paper collage (GPT Image 2.5).** Fully committed to its medium: torn edges showing a white core, fibres in the sky sheet, a crayon-spiral sun, wax scribbles on the kraft-paper sand, three torn floating islands, a hut and palms on the right. The ground is one long kraft strip with a white torn lip — unmistakable. Two faults: the sea is rows of white-crested torn waves directly behind the fighters, and the magenta hibiscus in three corners is the most saturated thing in the frame.
- **VK-D — Seaside candy kingdom (GPT Image 2.5).** A whipped-cream castle, a candy-striped pier, a pomegranate tree hanging into the frame, floating cake-islands veined with strawberry ripple, and an arched biscuit bridge for ground. The same watercolour and ink as A, finely drawn rather than loose. One of the best ideas in the batch is here: every walkable top is capped in white icing, so the walking line and the ledges are legible instantly. The worst is here too: the right-hand fighter, in cream and pink, vanishes into a cream and pink world.

### Arena readability

B and C read at a glance — a light flat top over a darker body, sea behind, sky above. D's ledges read just as fast thanks to the icing cap, but its palette is the characters' palette: Pomegraknight is pomegranate-red in a scene decorated with pomegranates, which breaks "a fighter reads first" (§2.6) unless the fruit is kept out of the fighting band. GPT's A fails: rocks, nets, stilts and pools at fighter height, and decks that are found only by studying the picture. Midjourney's A reads for a different reason — it is so pale and sparse that anything saturated pops — but only mj2 and mj3 give the player something to stand on. Nothing here confuses solid with air. The one volume question, where the sea starts to hold a body (§2.8), is answered by none of them: every image puts the sea behind the arena, not in it.

### Producibility

- **A as Midjourney paints it** is the cheapest change in the whole exploration, because the shipped sticker props are already this medium. The work: drop the white die-cut edge and let washes run past the line; replace tiled sand with a ground strip atlas — one wobbling ink line with a coral-earth wash fading below it (mj3); paint sky and sea as two or three full-width washes on bare paper for the far layers; planks, trestles, boat and huts as props with platform tops. These are hand- or AI-made sprites, as the beach kit already is — a code painter can do the wash bands but not the drawing. The grid costs it almost nothing: the picture is mostly air.
- **C** is the most kit-shaped direction in the batch. Collage *is* stamps: every piece may overlap and cast a small shadow, and torn edges, fibres and crayon scribble are within a code painter's reach (a noise-displaced outline with a white rim). Wave rows are ready-made parallax bands.
- **B** needs fully painted backdrops and painted ground pieces (rock bridge, shelves) — AI or hand, not code — and loses its light when cut up: the sun on the water is one gradient across the whole scene.
- **D** costs what A costs plus an entirely new prop set (castle, pier, cake-islands).

### Verdict

- **Pick: A, in Midjourney's handling (VK-A-mj2).** It answers the complaint at the least risk: the world keeps its medium and its shipped props, and the looseness comes from bare paper, running washes and a ground that is a line instead of a row of tiles.
- **Runner-up: C, torn-paper collage** — the choice if the first world should look unmistakably made by hand. Strongest kit fit; see the collision note at the end (three other worlds also offer a paper direction).
- **Carry over:** from D, the white icing cap on every walkable top and the pomegranate tree as the Vanilla Kingdom's own plant (kept out of the fighting band); from mj3, the coral-earth wash under the ground line; from B, the golden-hour palette and dark leaf frame as the look of a second map, not of the world.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj2 is the best of the four, mj3 second for its ground. Midjourney beats GPT Image on this direction — GPT's A is the more impressive painting and the worse answer.

## SL — Starland · Starfield

**Ships today:** clean flat vector — a banded violet-to-coral sky, sparkle stars, gold-capped pyramids, an obelisk with gold glyphs. The rigidity is plain to see: the big dolmen is a rectangle of brick courses on two brick legs, and the sand is a ruled strip with a repeating scalloped lip.

### Image by image

- **SL-A — Flat minimalist poster (GPT Image 2.5).** A dusk desert that is mostly sky: a half-buried colossal head with a hairline-gold diadem, a broken obelisk with a star-shaped hole and a slab jutting from its side, a stepped block, one floating rock, a teal crescent moon as the single cool accent. Every form splits into a lit ochre face and a plum shadow face along a hard edge, with print grain over it. It is richer than a strict two-plane poster and the sky is a painted gradient with a Milky Way, not posterised bands — but the emptiness, the scale and the warm-stone-on-cool-sky rule are all there. The fighters came out at about a twenty-fifth of the frame, less than half the size asked for.
- **SL-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** Truly flat: banded sky with gold-rimmed cloud scallops, a head in three planes, a chalice-shaped altar and a stepped plinth carrying a teal crystal. Clean and correct, and it looks like stock vector art; the five-point stars are sprinkled like a pyjama print. Thin dark outlines throughout, although the prompt said none.
- **SL-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four: a leaning obelisk pierced with star cut-outs, a teal stair-block, a tall obelisk, two thin slabs floating at different heights, a sweeping dune in front, sparkle stars of mixed sizes. Poster-flat and still a place.
- **SL-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The most arena-like: three floating slabs at three heights, gold kintsugi cracks on every stone — and a third fighter standing on a ledge, which nobody asked for. Stars are clip-art.
- **SL-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** The emptiest and calmest: head, a small teal obelisk, a small stepped plinth, two tiny fighters on a band of sand, three thin coral cloud bars. "Emptiness is the composition" taken literally; nothing to jump to.
- **SL-B — Risograph print (GPT Image 2.5).** Fluorescent coral and deep indigo overprinting to plum, real halftone dots, a textured gold sun. A crowned colossal head and a giant stone hand fade into the distance by dot density alone — the best depth device in the batch. Three dark floating slabs hung with emblem banners, a cliff-top ground, large fighters. Faults: it is a detailed illustration pushed through a riso filter, not flat shapes built from dots; the sky is hot coral with a single star, so Starland has lost its stars; the three identical banners are the repeated stamp returning.
- **SL-C — Painted star-chart mural (GPT Image 2.5).** The sky is a lapis ceiling with a gold zodiac ring — lion, bull, scorpion, archer, a sun at the keystone — over seated colossi, obelisks, a temple pylon and a moonlit lake; cracked plaster and flaking edges all round. The most original idea of the world, and the most crowded picture: the lower half slides into ordinary perspective painting, and the gilded ceiling fills exactly the space the fighters jump into. The banner-on-a-floating-slab formula appears again.
- **SL-D — Paper-cut shadow box (GPT Image 2.5).** Dark ruin layers against a coral-to-violet backlight: pyramids, a seated colossus, T-shaped ledges on crumbling stalks, an arched bridge for ground over water that mirrors the sunset, black reeds in front. Very readable — but it is not cut card. Surfaces are lumpy like clay, edges crumble instead of being knife-cut, and layers are shaded, so it reads as the familiar silhouette-platformer look.

### Arena readability

D is the clearest (dark shapes with a rim-lit top edge on a glowing ground), B next (the fighters are the darkest, sharpest shapes in the frame). A gives a simple rule a player learns in one screen: *the lit top plane is where you stand* — three ledges, each a light cap on a plum body, and nothing else at fighter height. C's ground slab and ledges are clear, but its sky is the highest-contrast, most ornate part of the picture and it sits where jumps happen; a fighter in warm colours disappears against gold figures, one in cool colours against lapis (§2.6). The hanging banners in B and C dangle into the fighting space and will be read as curtains or walls; drop them. No image confuses solid with air: far colossi fade, near stone is opaque.

### Producibility

- **A** is the same family as the current painter — flat planes — so a code painter can reach it: each monument one large prop built from two or three irregular polygons (lit face, shadow face, hairline gold), plus a grain pass; ground as a strip atlas with a lit cap and a plum body, its top profile a long non-repeating curve instead of a scallop; far layers are a sky gradient with a Milky Way, a sun, a row of mesas; near layer is dark plum dune and rock. Lost on the grid: the long diagonal dune ridge (ground stays level or uses the slope block) and the colossal scale — at the game's camera the head is a mid-distance prop, not a platform.
- **B** is unusually code-friendly: paint flat, then screen each ink into halftone and offset one plate. The dot screen must be aligned to the world, not to the tile, or dots will jump at every seam.
- **C** needs one large hand- or AI-made ceiling painting as a far layer; a code painter cannot draw a zodiac. The stone slabs under it are easy.
- **D** is the cheapest of all (flat dark shapes, a rim light, a gradient) and hides tile seams completely, but all its looseness would have to come from silhouette outlines.

### Verdict

- **Pick: A, GPT Image's version.** It is the style bible's Starfield with the bricks taken out: colossal irregular monoliths, a lit-plane rule for footing, and the emptiness that makes this the long-range world.
- **Runner-up: B, risograph** — only with the palette pulled back to an indigo night full of gold stars, and the detail flattened.
- **Carry over:** C's zodiac as a hairline-gold constellation ring in the far sky (the shipped kit already draws constellation lines; this is the same ornament grown up); mj1's gold-rimmed cloud bands; mj3's gold cracks on slabs; teal kept to one object per screen. Take nothing from D: flat dark silhouettes on a glowing sky are the Dark Wood's best option, and Starland should not look like it.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj2 is the best variant (mj3 if judged only as an arena). GPT Image wins this direction: Midjourney is flatter and closer to the letter of the brief, but it shows where the game already is — tidy vector — while GPT's A shows somewhere worth going. Midjourney's value here is as proof the cheap route works: swap the brick dolmen for three-plane monoliths and half the stiffness is gone.

## BM — Banboo Maze · Wulin 武林

**Ships today:** the v0.10.9 ink repaint. The natural things are already loose — black peaks bleeding into mist, pines, a pale moon. The built things are not: the pavilion is a ruled rectangle with a lattice window and hatched-grid roofs, the bridge and the bench are slabs, the ground is a block with a ragged hem, and the mist peaks along the bottom repeat as soft cones.

### Image by image

- **BM-A — Freehand ink 写意 (GPT Image 2.5).** A gorge in wet ink: a peak crowned with twisted pines and a thread of waterfall on the left, bamboo at the edge, three cantilevered crags with pale flat tops, a pavilion on a rock and an arched bridge on the right, a village lost in the mist, and for ground a long natural rock terrace that drips away into nothing. Splashed ink, dry flying-white, mist left as bare paper, a breath of cyan-green and no warm colour — the medium is carried. It is denser than true xieyi (more texture than stroke), and the crags are the model's stock floating island wearing ink. Two sword-fighters in ink and pale robes read perfectly against the white.
- **BM-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** A one-corner composition: peak and pine on the right, two stepped rock shelves reaching left into empty paper, a small pavilion and bridge between them, a village below. More than half the sheet is bare. Brush-pen contour with tinted wash, closer to an illustrated children's edition of Chinese painting than to splashed ink; the moss dots are as regular as peppercorns.
- **BM-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four: a gorge with a shelf jutting from the left peak, a high crag with the pavilion on the right, a lower shelf where the two fighters square up, a stone bridge and bamboo below, the village in mist between. Three ledges at three heights, all with clean pale tops. A vertical arena, which is what this world is.
- **BM-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The same kit with three small stones floating in mid-air as steps, one of them a mere dash. The stones are the weakest idea in the set — ink has no reason to float — but the left shelf, bamboo grove and pavilion crag are well drawn.
- **BM-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** Two shelves reach toward each other across a gap with a fighter on each: a duel across a chasm, the most wuxia staging in the batch. A pavilion crag above, a wooden bridge and village below. A little warm brown has crept into the timber.
- **BM-B — Blue-green landscape 青绿山水 (GPT Image 2.5).** Azurite and malachite peaks over ink contours on a tan silk ground with its weave showing, gold-line cloud bands, a gold-leaf sun, a wooden arched bridge to a pavilion, waterfalls, three turf-topped floating islands with pines, a grassy terrace for ground. The medium is faithfully done and it is sumptuous. It is also textured edge to edge — weave, texture strokes and gold everywhere — and the scenery is more saturated than the fighters.
- **BM-C — Night scroll, ink on indigo (GPT Image 2.5).** A huge cratered moon behind a pagoda, a dozen glowing waterfalls, lit lanterns on every ledge, bamboo silhouettes framing both sides. Dramatic, and not the medium asked for: light is rendered, not brushed — lantern glow, moon craters, volumetric mist — so it lands on generic dark-fantasy key art with ink spatter on top. Warm lantern light everywhere breaks this world's rule of no warm colour.

### Arena readability

A is the most readable direction in the whole exploration and the reason is the medium: paper is air, ink is solid, and nothing in the picture is saturated, so any fighter pops (§2.6). Both models put a pale flat top on every ledge; Midjourney's are the cleanest because there is nothing else on the sheet. B's ledges read (green cap on blue rock) but the fighters sit in front of busy mid-value tan-and-blue and lose their priority. C puts white waterfalls — the highest contrast in the frame — directly behind the fighters, and its dozen waterfalls make it impossible to tell which one would carry a body (§2.8): in Wulin the waterfall is a mechanic, so exactly one bold ribbon may sit in the play layer and far ones must stay threads. One caution for A as well: pine and bamboo are soft volume here, so they must stay light and open — Midjourney's bamboo does; GPT's dark pine masses look solid.

### Producibility

A continues what the v0.10.9 painter already does. What changes: ledges become cantilevered crags (one prop each, irregular outline, a pale flat top where the platform is); the ground becomes a long terrace that fades to paper well inside its canvas (§2.7); the pavilion and bridge are brushed in a few strokes instead of ruled. A code painter can do strokes and flying-white; the wet bleeding of GPT's image is harder, and ink is the easiest medium of all for AI- or hand-made sprites — black on white drops straight onto the paper canvas — provided solid crags keep an opaque paper backing so far peaks do not show through them (§2.4). Far layers: two or three ranges of dissolving peaks and the village as its own faint layer. The grid costs it only the unevenness of the walking line. B needs the silk ground as the canvas and painted peaks; a code painter could tint ink shapes but not lay mineral colour convincingly. C would need a full painted night backdrop.

### Verdict

- **Pick: A, GPT Image's ink with Midjourney's emptiness.** Keep the medium, brush the architecture, cantilever the ledges, and leave at least half the sheet bare.
- **Runner-up: B, but as a tint, not a ground:** white paper stays, and azurite and malachite go on far peaks only — which is what Midjourney's A variants already do.
- **Carry over:** mj4's duel across a gap as a map layout; the village-in-the-mist as a far layer; C's moon behind the peak for one night or boss map, painted in white ink on indigo with no lantern glow. Leave B's gold line out — Cloud Court and Starland own gold.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj2 is the best variant, mj4 second. GPT Image wins on medium (real wet ink against brush-pen line with regular dots); Midjourney wins on arena clarity. Use GPT's A as the look and mj2 as the measure of how little to paint.

## HC — HollowCastle · Dungeon

**Ships today:** the most rigid kit in the game. Ground, ceiling and walls are identical relief-shaded blocks in perfect running bond, the niches march at equal spacing, and the lava is a rectangle filled with a texture whose repeat is visible. It is also the least storybook of the nine — it reads as default game art, not as a picture-making tradition.

### Image by image

- **HC-A — Painterly lit realism (GPT Image 2.5).** A cavern of ruined arcades over lava: an arched bridge for ground, a masonry shelf with a brazier, a chunk of wall hovering in mid-air trailing chains, a timber gantry hung from a crane, lavafalls, cages, and a colossal seated knight in the gloom. Exactly the medium requested — no outline, brushwork, cold light from the upper left against orange bounce from below, grey stone with fire the only saturated colour. It is also the most generic picture in the batch: stock dark-fantasy concept art, grim where the cast is chibi, with fire-glow flooding nearly half the frame. In a realist medium the unsupported floating masonry looks like a mistake.
- **HC-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** Not the medium: thick black outlines and cartoon shading where the prompt said "no outlines, like an oil painting". What it does show is hand-drawn irregular masonry — no two stones alike — with planks on brackets, chain swags and a lava crack across the floor. Three children instead of two, standing on a floor seen from above.
- **HC-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four: big arches, a collapsed stair, four timber ledges on brackets at four heights, a cold shaft of light from the upper left and lava glow along the floor. Still outlined, still a floor in perspective and not a walking line.
- **HC-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** One great arch with a chain swag, three planks, a torch on its own bracket shelf, and the floor split by a fissure with fire in it — the best hazard staging of the set: the gap *is* the lava.
- **HC-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** A ruined turret inside a cavern with a three-storey timber scaffold against it and a lava pool at the foot of a broken stair. The scaffold is a ready-made multi-ledge prop.
- **HC-B — Prison etching with one fire colour (GPT Image 2.5).** Piranesi's prisons: tiers of barred arcades, a pulley wheel, drawbridges, a platform hung on chains, a hanging cage, and in the distance a chained giant slumped against the wall — the strongest single image idea in this world. Dense cross-hatching, cream plate tone, bitten blacks, and one hand-tinted orange for every fire. The medium is carried completely. The cost is noise: every surface is hatched at the same fineness, far tiers are drawn almost as firmly as near ones, and stairs that lead nowhere are a promise a platformer cannot keep.
- **HC-C — Dark fairy-tale gouache (GPT Image 2.5).** A crooked castle yard: leaning towers of mismatched stones, a drawbridge on chains, a cauldron slung between two pillars dribbling lava, a skeleton waving from behind bars, a mossy arched bridge over the fire. Opaque chunky paint, visible dry brush, orange rim-light from below. It has character and humour and it suits the cast. Two problems: it is outdoors under a moon with a lit castle on a hill — that is the Dark Wood's subject — and blue sky plus orange lava means the whole frame is saturated.
- **HC-D — Illuminated-manuscript hell (GPT Image 2.5).** A castle cut open into rooms: a forge with a demon smith, a gaol with skeletons, beams and ladders, arches full of flames drawn as curling tongues, a hell-mouth dragon breathing fire, a lapis sky sown with gold, a gold-leaf sun. Flat tempera, black contour, gold leaf, craquelure — the most original HC image and a medium fully carried. But the model turned the two fighters into scene characters (a haloed knight and a devil), added an angel and a Madonna-like saint in painted niches, which do not belong in this game, and pushed vermilion, ultramarine and gold to full strength everywhere.

### Arena readability

C reads best at game scale: big calm shapes, and every walkable top carries a green moss cap — the same trick as the Vanilla Kingdom's icing. Its blue-plumed fighter pops; the red-hooded one sinks into the orange haze. A's ground and ledges are found by their rim-lit edges, but fighters survive only as dark silhouettes, and anyone in warm colours would dissolve into the glow; the bible's rule that fire is the *only* saturated hue (§2.6) works when fire is a pool, not when it is the air. B has the strongest colour logic of all — black line, cream paper, and colour means heat — so a coloured fighter pops by construction, but the fine hatching at fighter height is busy, will shimmer when the camera moves, and its far architecture looks reachable. D's floor and beam tops are clear pale bands, but nothing is reserved for the actors: a red fighter in front of a vermilion niche is gone. Midjourney's outlined arches over black voids are the plainest statement of "solid is solid, air is air" (§2.4): mid-grey stone, near-black openings.

### Producibility

- **C** is within reach of a code painter: irregular stones of varied size with dry-brush fill and chipped corners, a moss cap on walkable tops, and a stylised orange rim on every underside. Because lava is always below in this world, that rim can be baked into the sprites. Crooked towers, the cauldron, the drawbridge and cages are props; ground is a strip atlas of bridge with arches as an under-layer; far layers are leaning silhouettes in cold blue-grey.
- **A** cannot be code-painted and cannot be baked honestly: its whole point is light that comes from where the lava is, so painted sprites go wrong the moment a prop moves. It needs hand- or AI-made sprites plus in-engine 2D lighting — the most expensive direction in the exploration.
- **B** is more approachable than it looks: hatching by rule (tone to line density, along the form) is a known technique, the single tint can be a glow layer drawn in-engine near any fire, and hatching hides seams. The hatch field must be world-aligned, and the far vaults and the giant must be drawn once as large far sprites. The shimmer risk has to be tested in motion before committing.
- **D** is the one direction where the grid is native — rooms, floors, beams and ladders are rectilinear by tradition — so it loses nothing to tiles. Contour and flat fill are code-paintable; gold leaf and craquelure are overlays; the figures and the dragon are not.
- **Midjourney's set** is the cheapest of all and is a kit sheet as it stands: irregular voussoirs and flagstones, gantries on brackets, chain swags, scaffold, broken stair.

### Verdict

- **Pick: C, dark fairy-tale gouache — brought indoors.** Crooked is the opposite of rigid, chunky paint stays calm behind a brawl, and it gives the Dungeon a storybook of its own. Take out the moon, the sky and the castle on the hill; let cold blue-grey stone replace the blue night so fire is again the only saturated colour.
- **Runner-up: B, prison etching** — the boldest medium here and the purest version of the bible's colour rule. Choose it only if the Dark Wood does not also take a hatched medium, and after a motion test.
- **Carry over:** A's cold key light against hot bounce and its palette discipline; Midjourney's kit (irregular masonry, gantries on brackets, the scaffold, the floor split by a lava fissure); B's chained giant and tiers of arcades as a far layer; D's flames as curling tongues for the lava's edge, and its cut-open castle as the layout of the boss arena.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj2 is the best variant. GPT Image wins on the medium — Midjourney ignored it and drew outlined cartoons — but Midjourney's four are the more useful pictures: they show what "less rigid" means stone by stone, and they sit closer to C than to A.

## TD — TheDeserted · Ghost Town

**Ships today:** a flat print on cream in ochre, rust, teal and black — a good-looking graphic. But the saloon is one large rectangle ruled with evenly spaced planks and nail dots, every façade is an axis-aligned box, and the black line is clean vector, not cut. It says "linocut" in its colours and "drawn with a ruler" in its shapes.

### Image by image

- **TD-A — Linocut relief print (GPT Image 2.5).** A real cut block: gouge marks in every solid, a rust sun with ink skipping across it, teal clouds dragged in horizontal cuts, mesas in rust hatch, a saguaro, a star sign hanging from a timber frame, a porch roof, a water tower, a two-storey saloon with a balcony and a covered wagon. The ground is a pale boardwalk strip over a deep band of carved black earth. The middle of the sheet is left as open cream paper and the two fighters — black shapes with one spot colour each — own it. Three or four inks, slightly off register. One of the two or three best images in the whole batch.
- **TD-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** A saloon with a striped awning and four slabs of earth floating beside it, two of them with a wagon parked on top. Clean cartoon line with a linocut veneer: no ink skips, no misregistration, and at least six colours where the medium allows three. It reads like a sticker sheet laid out on cream paper.
- **TD-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four, for one reason: the ground and the three floating ledges are solid black bars cut through with white gouge lines — the most linocut thing Midjourney drew, and an excellent way to say "this is solid". The building is still a tidy cartoon.
- **TD-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** A print with its own carved black border, large children in the foreground, one saloon, flat rust mesas. Print-like and pleasant, but it is a picture of a town, not an arena, and the border was forbidden.
- **TD-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** Three identical thin black bars step down like a staircase — the repeated stamp exactly. Worth noticing for one thing: the far mesas are drawn in key line only, with no colour block, which is a true print way to push something back.
- **TD-B — Frontier sketchbook (GPT Image 2.5).** Scratchy sepia pen and wash on foxed, deckle-edged paper with ink blots and a ring stain; a saloon corner with a crow on the balcony, buttes, a windmill, a little church, and a huge pale sky with one patch of faded blue. The loneliest picture of the four and well made. Its ledges are the same T-shaped timber frame three times over, each with a rag hanging from it, and the whole sheet is a single tan, so nothing is firmly near or far.
- **TD-C — Toy theatre (GPT Image 2.5).** A cardboard stage: red curtains in the corners, a painted backdrop of mesas, building fronts as flat card with white cut edges, a tumbleweed hanging on a thread, a boardwalk stage floor on a trestle, out-of-focus props in front. It looks like a photograph of a model and it is charming. The fighters are card stand-ups a little larger than asked. It is saturated and detailed from edge to edge, the painted backdrop included.
- **TD-D — Sun-bleached photograph, hand-tinted (GPT Image 2.5).** A grey-brown street receding to a church at the vanishing point, telegraph poles, timber awnings, a water tower on a bluff, with blue rubbed onto the sky and rust onto a door and an awning; scratches, stains and a rounded plate edge. It reads as a realistic 3D scene under an old-photo filter — the least hand-made image in the batch — and it is drawn in central perspective, which a side-scrolling arena cannot use. The plate edge is a border.

### Arena readability

A is excellent *as composed*: black is solid, cream is air, and the fighters stand in the one open field. The caution is this world's grammar — a two-storey street means fighters spend the match in front of façades, and A's saloon is dense carved black; a dark-outlined fighter in front of it would vanish. The shipped kit already knows the answer (a façade is one flat ochre ink) and it should be kept: heavy black carving for ground, platform bodies and frame edges; any wall the fight passes in front of in a single pale spot colour with few cuts. B and D are low-contrast monochromes: a coloured fighter would pop against either, but their own grounds and ledges are soft — B's walking line is a wash that fades, and D's is a grey ledge in a grey street. C's stage floor and shelf tops are clear, but the buildings and backdrop are as saturated as the actors (§2.6). The rags hanging from ledges in A and B dangle into the fighting band; cut them.

### Producibility

- **A** is the shortest road from what ships, and all of it is procedural: wobble every straight edge with noise; cut short white gouges into the black solids along the form; mask the spot inks with noise so they skip; offset each ink layer by a pixel or two, aligned to the world so the misregistration is the same everywhere. Break the box façades with sagging rooflines, leaning posts and uneven boards. Ground is a strip atlas — pale boardwalk on a carved black band (Midjourney's black bar is the simplest version). Far layers: mesas in one rust ink, hatch only; sun and cloud streaks behind; black wheel and cactus as a near layer. The wagon and water tower want better drawing than a painter gives — a few hand- or AI-made key blocks. Tiles cost it almost nothing: buildings are rectilinear by nature, and the looseness lives in the cut.
- **C** is a natural fit for a 2D engine — parallax layers *are* card layers, flat fronts are expected, white cut edges are an outline pass — but its charm is photographic: card thickness, soft shadows between layers and shallow focus. That means pre-blurred near and far layers and drop shadows per layer, and printed-card textures made by hand or AI.
- **B** needs hand- or AI-drawn sprites throughout; pen line this loose cannot be generated by rule, and it would not survive tiling.
- **D** needs photoreal assets and cannot keep its perspective. Not producible as a kit.

### Verdict

- **Pick: A, GPT Image's version.** The medium the world already has, finally cut by hand. It is the cheapest strong result in the exploration.
- **Runner-up: C, toy theatre** — the real departure, and one that turns flat façades and layers into the point. It would be the pick if the paper family were not already crowded (see the collision note).
- **Carry over:** Midjourney's solid black bar with white gouges as the look of ground and ledges, and mj4's key-line-only far mesas; B's emptiness — the huge pale sky, the crow, the little church and windmill as far silhouettes; C's tumbleweed on a thread as a prop gag. B itself should not be taken: pen line and wash is the Vanilla Kingdom's medium in brown.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj2 is the best variant. GPT Image wins outright — its A is a print; Midjourney's are cartoons coloured like one.

## PL — Palace of LOOING · Loong's Nest 龙宫

**Ships today:** deep indigo water with slanting light, three tiled roofs on banded purple pillars arranged like a symmetrical gate, pearl lamps, a faint palace behind. The even line and graded colour of gongbi are there; so are a ruled grid of roof tiles, dead-straight pillars, mirror symmetry, and a bubble column whose clusters repeat every few metres.

### Image by image

- **PL-A — Gongbi, fine line and heavy colour 工笔重彩 (GPT Image 2.5).** A dragon palace in full dress: tiers of pagoda roofs drawn tile by tile, a dragon coiled round a moon gate holding a pearl, a dragon statue, lilac coral trees, giant clams with glowing pearls, bubble strings; three scalloped clam-cap platforms on rock stems for ledges and an arched, shell-crusted bridge for ground. Fine even line and graded blues with no warm colour — the medium is honoured, in the manner of a lavish fantasy-game illustration. It is also filled from corner to corner at one mid-blue value, so the purple fighter all but disappears, and the model has painted waterfalls under the sea.
- **PL-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** Gongbi as a picture book: an even ink line, smooth graded teal and mauve, two halls on stone terraces joined by a stair, clams full of pearls, soft kelp, light falling from a rippled surface. The bubble lifts are drawn as glassy upright columns with bubbles inside — the clearest picture of "this column carries you" in the batch. Fault: a signboard with pseudo-Chinese lettering.
- **PL-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The most arena-like: a hall and stair on the left, two stacked terraces and a stair on the right, open water between, the two fighters squared up on a broad pale floor. Two lettered signboards on the right-hand hall.
- **PL-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four: the most open water, a shelf with a pavilion and a small floating slab at different heights, strings of pearl-bubbles rising through the gap, two clams, kelp passing in front of the floor as a near layer, no lettering. The fighters are tiny but perfectly clear on the pale terrace.
- **PL-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** Two rock terraces with a hall and a pavilion, big clams left and right, a pale floor with a scalloped underside hanging over a flat indigo deep. Pink coral and yellow lanterns have let warm colour in.
- **PL-B — Cave mural (GPT Image 2.5).** Dunhuang in spirit: malachite, azurite, earth red and chalk on abraded plaster, dragon-wrapped pillars at both edges, flying celestials trailing ribbons, clam-bowl ledges on rock stems, waterfalls drawn as striped ribbons. The medium is convincingly carried and nothing else in the game looks like it. Three serious problems: the two fighters are painted in the mural's own colours and outline and dissolve into it; the scene reads as a mountain landscape, not as water; and the model placed a large haloed Buddha figure at the top of the wall, which must not go into a fighting game's backdrop.
- **PL-C — Bioluminescent deep sea (GPT Image 2.5).** Black water lit by living light: palace domes like jellyfish bells, a dragon skeleton spanning the scene as a bridge, lantern-fish, three floating shelves each crowned by a glowing shell. A red scarf — the only warm thing in the frame — makes one fighter pop instantly. It is the most seductive image of this world and the least like a medium: glossy glow-on-black digital painting, the default look of AI fantasy art.

### Arena readability

Midjourney's A is the clearest: a pale flat terrace is always a place to stand, the dark rock under it is always body, and the open water is a smooth gradient with nothing in it but light and bubbles. GPT's A buries the same information in ornament; fighters in cool colours have nothing to stand out from (§2.6). B is camouflage, as rendered. C marks each ledge with a glowing shell — a good idea — but its solids are black on near-black and the hundreds of light points are noise; the walking line is a thin glimmer. On volumes (§2.8): this whole map is water, so the things that must be legible are the lifts and the currents. mj1's glass columns do the lifts properly; B's ribbon-drawn water is exactly how a current should be drawn; GPT's underwater waterfalls promise a physics the world does not have.

### Producibility

Gongbi is the medium a code painter does best — an even line and a smooth graded fill are what code draws — and the shipped kit is already built that way. Midjourney's version needs no new technique, only new shapes: terraces as irregular rock shelves with a flat pale top; halls with stairs, sized and placed asymmetrically; clam shells (radial ribs) and coral (branching) as procedural props; kelp as graded ribbons, soft volume, swaying; bubble columns as glassy shafts exactly as wide as the lift, with bubbles that do not repeat. Far layers: surface ripple and light shafts, hazed palace silhouettes, one dragon gate as a set-piece; kelp as the near layer. The grid costs it nothing — every walkable thing is flat-topped — and stairs can use the slope block. GPT's A at its own density is hand- or AI-sprite work and too busy to want. B needs painted wall sections and a weathering overlay. C is cheap in-engine (dark shapes plus additive glow sprites).

### Verdict

- **Pick: A, in Midjourney's handling (PL-A-mj3), a shade darker.** Keep the fine line and graded colour, break the symmetry, build from terraces, shells and kelp. Pull the water back toward the bible's deep indigo and keep pearls the only lamps.
- **Runner-up: C, bioluminescent** — the crowd-pleaser. It is a lighting idea, not a medium, and its cyan-and-violet glow on black is The Forgotten's neon without the pixels; take it only knowing that.
- **Carry over:** from GPT's A, the dragon coiled round the moon gate as the far set-piece, and scalloped clam-caps as a ledge shape; from C, the dragon's ribcage as a bridge and the rule that the brightest thing on a ledge is its edge; from B, water currents drawn as flowing ribbons — and nothing figurative; from mj1, the glass bubble column.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj3 is the best variant, mj2 second if its signboards are painted out. Midjourney beats GPT Image here: the same medium with a third of the ornament, and an arena you can read.

## KH — Kingdom of Horror · Dark Wood

**Ships today:** blue line on a navy board — bare trees with a moonlit edge, a black castle with yellow windows, a yellow hatched moon, a tree with a jack-o'-lantern face. The trees are organic and good. The platforms are one sprite repeated six times with the same root pattern under each, the two bramble thickets are the same scribble, and the line is smooth vector, not scratched.

### Image by image

- **KH-A — Scratchboard (GPT Image 2.5).** The truest scratchboard in the batch: everything, sky and cloud included, is fine white-blue hatching scratched from black, thick where the moon falls and absent in shadow. Two enormous tree-faces flank the scene, a rope bridge, crooked branch ledges, a castle with one yellow window against a scratched moon, yellow will-o'-the-wisps, red thorns and red bear traps. The colour code is obeyed exactly. It is also grim — a metal-album cover, with pools of blood under the traps and a skull on a stake — and murky: the fighters are small dark shapes in a dark middle distance.
- **KH-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** A friendlier reading: trunks and brambles drawn in long white scratch lines on black, stern-faced trees, four shelves in a two-by-two arrangement joined by rope bridges, yellow wisps, red lantern-traps hanging under the lower shelves, the castle and moon in a pale gap of mist. Clear and playable; the layout is as symmetrical as a tile map, and the mist is airbrushed, not scratched.
- **KH-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four: trees whose eyes glow yellow, rope bridges sagging across the gap, two shelves and a stepped hill up to the castle door, tangles of bramble in the corners, wisps drifting in pale fog. Asymmetrical, alive, and every ledge is findable.
- **KH-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The most open: three shelves with icicle drips, a black hill and castle, a big moon, three wisps. One of the two fighters is bright red, which the world's own code reserves for danger.
- **KH-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** A taller arena — shelves at three heights hung with drips, rope bridges, a red glowing lantern-creature in the corner — with two little shadow-children for fighters, black with white eyes. The floor is a mat of scratched roots.
- **KH-B — Victorian gothic pen and ink (GPT Image 2.5).** Cross-hatched pen under grey-violet wash: a gnarled tree with crows and a lantern, a graveyard, iron gates and a gargoyle, floating turf islands with pumpkins, a spired castle against the moon, jack-o'-lanterns glowing orange everywhere. Cosy-spooky, well drawn and right for a pumpkin king. It is busier and more ordinary than the deadpan stillness the brief asked for — a Halloween picture book — and it is line-and-wash, the Vanilla Kingdom's medium in mourning.
- **KH-C — Silhouette shadow theatre (GPT Image 2.5).** Pure black scissor-cut shapes — filigree branches with leaves, hanging birdcages, mushrooms, a root bridge — in front of crumpled tissue lit from behind, blue through violet to a huge orange moon; far pines and castles are paler tissue silhouettes; on a balcony at the top right stands a pumpkin-headed king with crown and sceptre. The medium is carried completely and it is the most beautiful image of this world. Its weak spot is the left third, where black sits on deep blue.

### Arena readability

C is close to ideal: black is solid, light is air, and there is nothing in between (§2.4). It even offers a code for volumes (§2.8): a thicket can be cut as lace — holes let light through, so it looks enterable — fog can be a pale veil of tissue, and a trap can sit in a red glow. Coloured fighters on black shapes will always be the most saturated thing on screen (§2.6); the backlight only has to stay bright behind the fighting band, which the image's left side does not manage. Midjourney's A reads nearly as well, because it puts a pale field of mist behind the action: black trees with white edges, pale air. GPT's A, the more faithful scratchboard, is the hardest to read — hatching everywhere means no rest for the eye and no bright field for a fighter to stand against. B is clear in its misty centre and cluttered at its ledges, which trail roots and rags.

### Producibility

- **C** is among the cheapest kits in the exploration. The play layer is flat black — ground strip with a grass-tuft lip, branch ledges, trees, cages — which a code painter draws easily and which hides every tile seam; the existing tree painter already makes the branching. Behind it: one full-screen gradient with a crumpled-tissue texture and halos round the lights; two or three far ranges as paler translucent silhouettes; black roots as a near frame. The craft is all in the outline, so the cut shapes need designing with care. The grid costs nothing.
- **A, as Midjourney paints it,** continues the shipped painter: replace smooth strokes with bundles of long scratch lines, give each ledge its own outline and drips, hang rope bridges as one-way platforms, grow the bramble as tangles instead of a stamped ball, and put pale mist behind the play band. Faces on trees are hand or AI work.
- **GPT's A** would need every sprite hatched to its lighting — hand or AI — and fine white hatching on black will shimmer in motion.
- **B** is hand- or AI-drawn throughout and would not tile.

### Verdict

- **Pick: C, silhouette shadow theatre.** The most readable, the cheapest to build well, a real storybook tradition for a fairy-tale horror world — and it removes an overlap the other choices create: white line cut out of black is what both scratchboard and linocut are.
- **Runner-up: A in Midjourney's handling (KH-A-mj2)** — the world's own medium, loosened and friendlier. Choose it to keep the shipped kit and painter; if so, keep Ghost Town's blacks from becoming white-lined too.
- **Carry over:** A's colour code into C — backlight stays moonlight blue and violet, yellow and orange only where something gives light, red glow only for danger; tree-faces as cut-outs whose eyes are holes with light behind (mj2); sagging rope bridges; B's pumpkin patch, gates and crows as silhouettes. Leave out GPT A's blood.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj2 is the best variant, mj1 second. Midjourney wins for this game: GPT Image made the truer scratchboard, but Midjourney made the one a chibi fighter can be seen in.

## TF — The Forgotten · Forgotten City

**Ships today:** true low-resolution pixel art and very sparse: one flat violet pyramid with dotted cyan edges, three identical thin slabs with a cyan under-glow, a glyph pillar, a rectangle of dithered swamp, earth whose texture repeats. Pixels make a grid honest, so this world is the least wrong to be rigid — its stiffness is emptiness and repetition, not the medium.

### Image by image

- **TF-A — Hard pixel art (GPT Image 2.5).** A night city over a swamp: stepped pyramids with thin cyan and magenta lines running down their stairs, a causeway lined with small flames, carved serpent heads pouring teal water, three mossy floating slabs each carrying glyphs in a single neon colour, pillars standing in the water, reeds and marigolds in front, a mossy arched causeway for ground with neon mirrored in the water under it. Deep, layered, and the neon really is thin. The pixel discipline is looser than asked — more colours than a fixed palette, soft halos round the flames, pixels of more than one size — but at a glance it is good 16-bit art. The two fighters, in gold and green, are the warmest things in a cool frame and read at once.
- **TF-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** Stricter pixels than GPT's: one grid, a fixed palette, bold outlines, an ordered-dither band on the horizon, no glow. But the swamp is drawn from above like a role-playing-game town map, with a side-view strip of bricks glued along the bottom; the four mossy slabs are one stamp; and the dusk is light and cheerful, not a forgotten city at night.
- **TF-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four for its kit: ledges that grow out of the pyramids on carved-face brackets, with curled vine tips, plus small floating turf slabs, stone beasts at the water, thin neon on the stair edges. The river is still a top-down map.
- **TF-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** Two pyramids mirrored about a central canal, two long beams across the top, two stone tables, and three children on a brick embankment. The most symmetrical image in the batch — the rigidity we are trying to leave, in pixels.
- **TF-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** The cleanest layout: four tan slabs at four heights in an open violet sky, pyramids at the sides, water edged with cyan ripples that read like neon. The slabs and the brick floor are plainly repeated blocks.
- **TF-B — Lush modern pixel art (GPT Image 2.5).** A festival night: a pink moon, pyramids with lit windows, huge glowing cyan and magenta glyphs mirrored in a lagoon, papel-picado strung across the sky, braziers on every ledge, canoes, glowing lotuses. Lovely, and no longer quite pixel art — it is a glowing digital painting seen through a pixel filter. The neon is not thin, there are thirty light sources, and the fighters are among the dullest things in the frame.
- **TF-C — Painted codex with circuitry (GPT Image 2.5).** A page of bark paper: flat colour inside black contour, temples in profile, a half-faced sun, the lagoon as bands of turquoise scrolls, a feathered serpent lying across the scene as a ledge, a great pale axolotl spirit in the water, and cyan circuit traces running through the dark ground and the left wall. Every walkable top carries a red cap. The medium is fully carried and the picture is a delight. The model redrew the fighters as codex warriors, the moon glyph came out as a crescent holding a star, and on tan paper the "neon" is only a thin turquoise line.

### Arena readability

A is the best-read dark image in the exploration: a green moss cap on every walkable top, slabs darker than the haze behind them, a background that is busy but low in contrast, and warm fighters on a cool field (§2.6). The swamp sits below the causeway with a clear surface line and reflections — it looks like water you would fall into (§2.8), which is this world's whole grammar. B spends the frame's contrast and saturation on fire and glyphs. C's red caps make its ledges the easiest in the world to find, and the pale lagoon band is a calm field behind the fighters; but that lagoon is drawn as backdrop at fighter height, so nothing says where the water you can drown in begins. All four Midjourney images mix a top-down middle with a side-view floor: there are ledges, but no arena.

### Producibility

Pixel art is the most code-friendly medium of the nine: any shape a painter can draw can be rendered small, snapped to a fixed palette and shaded by ordered dither, and it will be correct pixel art by construction. A needs richer shapes, not a new technique — moss and vines that hang below the tile line, cracks, slabs of varied length, pillars in the water, torches along a far causeway, two or three hazed pyramid layers — plus a few hand-pixelled or cleaned-up AI set-pieces (the serpent heads). AI pixel art must always be re-snapped to one true grid and one palette before use; GPT's image is not usable as it stands. The neon reflections can be a mirrored, dithered strip. Tiles cost nothing here. B's light cannot be painted into tiles; it would need glow and reflection in a shader, or very large hand-made scenes. C would need hand- or AI-drawn glyph figures throughout.

### Verdict

- **Pick: A, GPT Image's scene held to Midjourney's pixel discipline.** The hero of this world is called Pixolotl; the world is pixel art. A shows that hard pixels can be deep, mossy and irregular.
- **Runner-up: B** — only as a few borrowed effects on A's sprites (mirrored neon in the water, a mist layer), not as a rendering style.
- **Carry over from C, all of it as pixel murals and props:** the feathered serpent as a bridge, the axolotl spirit under the water, codex figures and circuit borders carved on the pyramid walls, and a single-colour lip on every walkable top. From A: one neon colour per slab. From mj2: ledges on carved brackets. From B: papel-picado in a far layer, well above the fight.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj2 is the best variant, mj4 the cleanest layout. GPT Image wins — it drew the forgotten city, from the side, at night; Midjourney drew a tidier pixel grid in the wrong projection and the wrong mood.

## CC — Cloud Court

**Ships today:** real cloisonné colour — gold wire, mottled jewel enamel, a violet night — arranged with a set square: a square moon-gate panel, a rectangle of gold lattice for ground, two identical dragon columns in mirror image, one cloud stamp used four times, a sea of repeating scales. After the Dungeon it is the most ruled kit in the game. (It also has no world: its map is played from the map editor.)

### Image by image

Gold line encloses everything, but the cells are filled with marbling and a web of gold veins — it is closer to gold-veined stone or mended lacquer than to flat enamel cells. Spectacular for five seconds and exhausting after: there is no rest anywhere, and bright cloud fills the frame, so nothing tells a playable cloud from scenery.
- **CC-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** True cloisonné: flat mottled enamel in cells, raised gold wire, cloud scrolls that end in wire spirals, red-roofed pavilions, a green dragon looking over a cloud, lotus and pearls. It is a decorative panel, though — bands of ornament with two children standing in the middle — and it has a gold frame round it, which the prompt forbade.
- **CC-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** An arena: a deck of teal, lapis and cinnabar bands for ground, a palace terrace on the right with a dragon wound round the building, a small pavilion on the left, an arched bridge behind, cream cloud scrolls in front, open night sky above.
- **CC-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four: the most open sky, terraces on lotus pads at three heights with curved cinnabar undersides, a floor of imperial yellow that both fighters read against, a small dragon by the stair, cream clouds only at the edges. Every top is flat and every cell is clean.
- **CC-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** Darker and busier, with two floating red slabs whose undersides are gold lattice — the shipped kit's rectangles again. Four children instead of two, and a gold frame.
- **CC-B — Embroidered silk (GPT Image 2.5).** A hanging of midnight-blue silk: padded white clouds in raised satin stitch, floating islands whose rock is dense green-grey thread under a pale stitched top, red knotted tassels, a red-pillared pavilion, a rope bridge, three cranes, a couched-gold moon carrying a far palace, waterfalls in white thread. Thread direction, sheen and padding are all there — the medium is carried completely. And the plain blue silk between the motifs is the calmest field in the whole exploration: both fighters stand against it and nothing competes. One of the best images in the batch.
- **CC-C — Blue-and-white porcelain 青花 (GPT Image 2.5).** The scene painted in cobalt on the swell of a great jar — its shoulder band at the top, glossy highlights at the edges, gold-mended cracks across it: a dragon in cloud, floating islands with dark cobalt undersides, pines, pavilions, an arched rock bridge. Beautiful, and well observed as porcelain. The fighters were painted in cobalt too, as part of the jar. The trouble is what it is: brush landscape in one colour on a white ground, with pines, pavilions and mist — Wulin, in blue.

### Arena readability

This world's own rule is that the backdrop is tinted toward the night so that only playable clouds are bright (§2.6), and clouds here are soft volume while footing is scarce (§2.8). B obeys it best: plain silk is air, a padded white cloud looks soft and raised — exactly what an enterable cloud should look like — and stitched rock with a pale top looks firm. It would obey it completely if far clouds were stitched flat in dark thread, tone on tone, leaving white padding for clouds that can be entered. mj3 obeys it too: a violet night, bright things only where there is something to stand on. GPT's A breaks it everywhere. C reads as clearly as ink does, and for the same reason. The tassels under B's islands hang into the fighting band and are red; shorten them or lose them.

### Producibility

- **A as Midjourney paints it** is the cheap route. The shipped painter already makes cells, relief wire and mottling. It needs new shapes only: clouds as free scrolls ending in wire spirals instead of one stamp; terraces with curved undersides; balustraded bridges and stairs; lotus pads as platforms; a deck strip atlas in bands of enamel. The dragon is a hand- or AI-made far set-piece. Decks are flat and wire is at least a pixel wide, so the grid and the game's resolution cost it nothing.
- **B** is the same structure with a different surface — outline plus filled shape plus relief — so the existing painter can be adapted: fill each shape with fine parallel strokes along a direction, add a band of sheen, couch a twisted gold line round it, emboss the clouds. The silk weave is the canvas and must be aligned to the world. What will not survive is the photographic look: at the game's resolution thread becomes one-pixel hatching. That is still embroidery, but it should be tested before committing.
- **C** could be painted by Wulin's ink painter with the colour changed, which is the proof that it is the same medium.

### Verdict

- **Pick: B, embroidered silk.** Soft where enamel is hard, so it answers "too rigid" at the root; it gives fighters the cleanest field in the game; it draws a cloud that looks enterable; and it puts real distance between Cloud Court and Loong's Nest, which are otherwise two outlined Chinese palaces in cool colours.
- **Runner-up: A in Midjourney's handling (CC-A-mj3)** — the bible's medium with the set square taken away, at a fraction of the cost. Since this world is not in the pool, doing mj3 now and keeping B for the day it gets a world is a sound order.
- **Carry over:** the dragon coiling through cloud as the far set-piece (A); gold kept, as couched thread — it is the same gold line the kit already has; lotus-pad terraces and the imperial-yellow floor (mj3); cranes as the world's moving ornament (B). From C, nothing as a world — at most one novelty map painted on a vase.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** mj3 is the best variant, mj2 second. Midjourney beats GPT Image on this direction: it made cloisonné, GPT Image made glitter.

## OW — Overworld (the run map)

Judged by different questions from the arenas: does it read as a *map* at a glance; would it still read when the island is generated differently every run (five river-divided realms round a central black pentagon, nodes and dotted roads); does each realm keep its own medium; and what would code have to draw. One thing to hold in mind throughout: the images show five realms (VK, BM, TD, KH, TF), while the pool has eight. Starland's poster, the Dungeon and Loong's Nest have not been tried on any of these maps.

### Image by image

- **OW-A — One cartographer, five inks (GPT Image 2.5).** A parchment sheet with a compass rose, an ornamental border, ripple hatching round a lobed coast, ships and sea-stacks; a scorched black pentagon at the centre and five blue rivers cutting five wedges. Each wedge really is in its own medium — pink watercolour kingdom, ink-wash peaks and bamboo with bare paper, rust-and-ochre mesas and a frontier town, a blue-black scratched wood with orange pumpkin lights, pixel pyramids and canals. It is a map before it is anything else. Two faults: the nodes and dotted roads are there but tiny, and vanish entirely in the dark wood; and the pixel realm came out as a bright green daytime jungle, the loudest thing on the sheet.
- **OW-A-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** A cleaner, emptier pictorial map with bold stars and dots for nodes. But the wedges are divided by ruled straight lines like slices of pie while the rivers wander and branch somewhere else — the rivers do not divide anything. All five realms are drawn by one cartoon hand in five palettes.
- **OW-A-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** Covered in garbled lettering ("KINGDEN", "GHONT CONALS") although the prompt forbade text, with four compass roses. Discard.
- **OW-A-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four and the best *map* of all eleven: five blue rivers are the borders, the pentagon is scorched at the edges, stars and dots and dotted roads sit boldly on bare parchment, and each realm is a small vignette with room round it. Its weakness is the media: the ink realm is ink and the wood is cut like a print, but the pyramids and the desert are plain outlined cartoon. Four compass roses.
- **OW-A-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** The centre is a five-pointed star, not a pentagon, and there is one river instead of five. The wire-frame green pyramids are a nice hint of neon. Discard as a test of the rule.
- **OW-B — Torn storybook pages (GPT Image 2.5).** Five torn pages fitted into a disc on a sea of crumpled teal paper whose torn white edges are the wave crests; strips of blue paper show through the tears as rivers; the pentagon is burnt through the middle with charred, curling edges. Each page is in its own medium *on its own paper* — watercolour paper, rice paper, cream print stock, black board, dark paper for the pixels — which is the strongest separation of realms in the set. Each page is a full scenic illustration with its own sky. The nodes are beads on thread, and too small to find.
- **OW-B-mj1 (Midjourney v8.2).** Five torn scraps with one simple scene each, wide blue paper rivers, cut-paper waves, a few pins. The scraps float apart, so there is no island. The centre is a star — as it is in all four of these variants; Midjourney would not draw a pentagon.
- **OW-B-mj2 (Midjourney v8.2).** Round-headed pins joined by twine: the best node-and-road language in the set, legible on any page however dark. Large pages, wide rivers, no island outline. Star.
- **OW-B-mj3 (Midjourney v8.2).** The best of the four: a disc island ringed by a paler lagoon, strips of cut-paper wave all round, pins and thread, five clear pages each on its own paper. Star.
- **OW-B-mj4 (Midjourney v8.2).** A clean disc of cream card lies under the pages, so the island keeps its outline even where pages do not meet — a useful trick. Few pins. Star.
- **OW-C — Board-game table (GPT Image 2.5).** A photographed tabletop model: pink ceramic, carved pale wood, tooled leather, black lacquer and mosaic tile, rivers of blue resin, a black glass pentagon, and wooden discs on dotted roads — the clearest node graph of all eleven. But it is a 3D render seen at a slant with props in the corners; the desert piece became a domed palace instead of a ghost town; and five materials are not five media. The whole map is now one medium: miniature photography.

### Does it read as a map; does it survive generation

- **A** reads as a map instantly and survives any island: paper, border, compass, coastline and hatching do not care what shape the land is, and each realm is a ground treatment plus stamps scattered inside its region. Its weak point is dark realms — the wood here, and the Dungeon, Starland's night and the Forgotten City when they come up — which meet across nothing but a thin river and swallow the nodes.
- **B** reads as an island of five pictures, and as a map only once the pins are on it; the node layer has to do the map's work. It survives generation better than it first appears, for one reason: **a torn page is supposed to cut through its picture**. A castle sliced by a region boundary is a bug on a cartographer's sheet and exactly right on a torn page. A tear can follow any boundary, it gives every realm a white lip for free so two dark pages never merge, and a pin can be stuck anywhere on a page whatever is printed under it.
- **C** reads as a board with routes, which is close to a map. It survives generation worst: every run needs bevelled pieces of a new shape, and it needs eight materials invented for eight worlds.

### What code would have to draw

- **Shared layers, any direction:** the sea; the coastline; rivers from the generator's five boundaries; the pentagon; nodes and roads with a pale halo so they hold on any ground; one compass.
- **A adds** per realm: a ground treatment clipped to the region and six to ten landmark stamps in the realm's medium, scattered by rule and kept clear of roads. The realm painters in `Tools/realm_art/` can draw their own stamps. This is the least work.
- **B adds** to that: a torn-edge mask along every boundary (a noise-displaced edge, a white fibrous lip, a soft shadow); a paper stock per realm (colour and grain); rivers as gaps with blue paper behind; a cut-paper sea; a card disc under the pages (mj4) to hold the coastline; and the burn — a charred, curling edge that spreads outward from the pentagon as the VOID devours. Start with stamps on paper, exactly as in A. A full illustrated page per realm, clipped by the tear, can come later and needs no new code — it is one texture and the same mask.
- **C** needs material shaders, bevels from each region's shape, consistent lighting and modelled tokens. It is the most work and the least drawn.

### Verdict

- **Pick: B, torn storybook pages.** It is the game's own premise as a picture — worlds torn out of different storybooks — and it makes each realm's medium physical by giving it its own paper. Tears are rivers, the burn is the VOID, and both mean something a player will feel when the burn spreads. Built on A's rule set, it costs a mask, a paper and a burn more than A does.
- **Runner-up: A, in Midjourney's handling (OW-A-mj3)** — the safe choice, and the fallback if the torn edges do not read at the size the map is played at.
- **Carry over into B:** from mj3, the restraint — a few landmarks and bare paper on every page, so that pins are the brightest things on the map; from OW-B-mj2, pins and twine as nodes and roads (or C's wooden discs, which read even better); from A, one compass and ripple hatching where the sea meets the card; from GPT's B, the charred pentagon and the torn white wave crests.
- **Midjourney vs GPT Image:** for A, mj3 is the best variant and beats GPT Image as a map, while GPT Image beats it at keeping five media. For B, mj3 is the best variant, but GPT Image wins: it drew a pentagon, real torn pages and real media. The recipe is GPT Image's pages with Midjourney's pins and Midjourney's restraint.

## Do the picks still make nine different books?

Yes. Together they are nine ways of making a picture: watercolour and pen (VK), flat planes (SL), brush and bare paper (BM), opaque gouache (HC), a cut block (TD), fine line and graded colour (PL), scissor-cut silhouette against light (KH), pixels (TF), thread (CC). No two could trade a tile by swapping palettes, which is the test in §0, and the overworld pick frames them as what they are — pages from different books. The places where it could break, most likely first:

1. **Dark Wood against Ghost Town.** If KH takes A instead of C, scratchboard and linocut are both white marks cut out of black. They can be kept apart — hair-thin tangled line everywhere, against thick gouges with flat spot inks and bare cream — but C removes the problem.
2. **Loong's Nest against Cloud Court.** If CC stays cloisonné in Midjourney's handling, it sits beside a Loong's Nest in Midjourney's handling as a second outlined Chinese palace with smooth fills. Enamel must then keep what gongbi cannot have: raised gold wire, flat mottled cells, cinnabar and imperial yellow.
3. **The paper family.** VK-C, SL-D, TD-C and KH-C are all paper craft, and OW-B is paper too. One arena world in paper inside a paper overworld is a theme. Three would turn "different media" into "different scissors". Take at most one of them besides the overworld.
4. **Dungeon against Vanilla Kingdom.** HC-C has to stay opaque and free of outline. With a pen line round every stone — which is how Midjourney drew the Dungeon — it becomes the Beach at night.
5. **Starland against Dark Wood.** Both put flat shapes on a glowing sky. Starland's stones always show two lit planes and never go to pure silhouette; the Dark Wood's backlight stays cold blue, with orange only where something gives light.
6. **Runner-ups that are another world in disguise.** CC-C is Wulin in blue. PL-C is the Forgotten City's neon without the pixels. TD-B and KH-B are the Vanilla Kingdom's pen and wash in brown and in violet. HC-B with KH-A and TD-A would be three hatched monochromes with one accent colour each.

Six of the nine picks are night scenes. That is palette, not medium, but it is a reason to hold Starland at coral dusk and the Dark Wood at moon-blue so that the worlds differ at thumbnail size.

## What needs deciding besides the picks

1. **Grid and collision.** Recommended: collision stays on the 2 m grid and the art leaves it. Each ledge becomes one large prop with an irregular outline and a flat walking line on its canvas top (§2.2). Ground becomes a strip atlas whose top line is exact and whose body is irregular. Art may overhang its footprint — roots, drips, the underside of a crag — wherever it is not solid. The alternative, one painting per map with collision placed by hand, looks best and breaks the map editor and the five maps every pool world ships. One point to settle explicitly: may a walking line be uneven? Recommended no — flat, or the slope block — with the unevenness painted below the line and in a near layer.
2. **Who paints.** The bible's rule is code painters (§1). TD, SL, PL, TF, KH-C and CC can stay code-painted. VK is already hand-made. Wulin's wet ink, the Dungeon's set-pieces, dragons, serpent heads and tree-faces want hand- or AI-made sprites. Decide whether AI-made sprites may enter a kit, and who cleans them (edges per §2.7; pixel snapping for TF).
3. **Resolution.** The game draws 32 px per metre, so a fighter is 64 px tall; the concepts show about one and a half times that much detail. Bold media survive (poster, silhouette, linocut, pixels, enamel). Fine ones — thread, hatching, halftone, flying-white — blur unless kits go to 64 px per metre. Decide this before anything is repainted.
4. **Fighters keep one house style.** The models redrew them in each medium (cobalt, codex figures, card stand-ups). "A fighter reads first" only works if they stay the same everywhere.
5. **The light cap on walkable tops** as a rule for every kit, and **nothing hanging into the fighting band** (rags, banners, tassels).
6. **Order.** Pilot one world end to end before committing the rest. TD-A is the right pilot: all procedural and the cheapest strong result. The Dungeon next, where the gain is largest. Cloud Court last, since it has no world.
